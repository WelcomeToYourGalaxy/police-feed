What this is. A feed on policing worldwide, in twenty-four subjects: the duty to protect and its absence, immunity and the code of silence, corruption and extortion, criminal infiltration of the force, use of force and militarisation, custody before charge, torture and coerced confession, enforced disappearance and death squads, border and immigration enforcement, transnational repression, private and parallel policing, counter-terrorism used as political policing, who gets policed, the export of training and equipment, sexual violence by officers, the surveillance apparatus from facial recognition to wide-area drones, bulk data and who buys it, informants inside movements, stops and searches and the rights that answer them, grand juries and compelled testimony, disarmament, the privacy tools that turned out to be compromised, and the reforms and refusals set against all of it.

A crime report is not a subject. A robbery, a manhunt, a traffic collision are crime stories and are refused, and so is what happens after charge — sentencing, parole, prisons and trials belong to the courts and corrections wire. This one stops where the police hand over. What they did, were permitted to do, or were not held to account for is what it keeps, and every subject term is gated behind a policing or accountability term so that the wire cannot fill with blotter.

Weight. Separately, each story is scored on what it contains: a decision — a ruling, a law enacted, a referendum result, a conviction (2); institutional material — WHO or UN guidance, official statistics, a health ministry, a peer-reviewed study (2); a measured figure (1); a pending decision with a date attached (1); a named jurisdiction (1); and a primary source (1). At three or more the row is marked consequential, and the Weight filter narrows to those. A campaign statement and a constitutional judgment both appear here; the pips are how you tell them apart at a glance, and the words beside them say what earned the score.

What is refused. The word abort in its other senses — aborted launches, landings, mergers and transactions — plus the usual commercial and horoscope noise. The status line reports how many stories each harvest refused.

Translation. The Translate control at the top renders the headlines and snippets on screen into any of 26 languages, through a free public translation endpoint — no account, no key, nothing stored. A translated row is marked as machine translation and keeps the publisher's original wording on hover, because a machine rendering is not the publisher's words. If the endpoint is unavailable the row simply stays in its original language rather than emptying. Translation applies to what is on screen; scroll further or widen a filter and press the control again.

Region. Placement runs three levels deep. Pick a region and a second row opens with its subregions; pick one of those and a third row lists the places inside it — Latin America to the Amazon Basin to the Brazilian Amazon, Oceania to Australia to the Northern Territory. Each row counts what it holds, so an empty subregion is visible rather than hidden, and every story files under the subregion and region above whatever place it names.

Limits worth knowing. The gate is mechanical: it reads words, not meaning. Standing is assigned per wire rather than per article, so a straight news piece from an advocacy outlet still carries that outlet's label, and a slanted piece from a wire service does not. The balance of the feed depends on the balance of the source list, which is in sources_police.json and is meant to be argued with — it reads the oversight bodies and courts beside Amnesty, Human Rights Watch, the EFF, the ACLU, Privacy International, Statewatch, the Citizen Lab, the Center for Constitutional Rights and the Marshall Project. Google News caps a query at roughly 100 results over about 30 days. Coverage is uneven by language and the counts show it rather than hiding it.

Every wire read on the last harvest, with its standing: what it returned, and how many of its stories were refused. A wire that could not be reached says so rather than disappearing.
