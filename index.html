What this is. A feed on policing worldwide, in twenty-four subjects: the duty to protect and its absence, immunity and the code of silence, corruption and extortion, criminal infiltration of the force, use of force and militarisation, custody before charge, torture and coerced confession, enforced disappearance and death squads, sexual violence by officers, the surveillance apparatus from facial recognition to wide-area drones, bulk data and who buys it, privacy tools and the ones that turned out to be compromised, the export of training and equipment, informants inside movements, counter-terrorism used as political policing, transnational repression, private and parallel policing, who gets policed, border and immigration enforcement, stops and searches and the rights that answer them, compelled testimony, disarmament, and the reforms and refusals set against all of it. Nothing here is written or summarised: headlines and snippets are the publishers' own, every row links to the original, and no model touches the text.

What it is not. A crime report is not a subject. A robbery, a manhunt, a traffic collision are crime stories and are refused. So is what happens after charge — sentencing, parole, prisons and trials belong to the courts, not here. This wire stops where the police hand over. What they did, were permitted to do, or were not held to account for is what it keeps, and every subject term is gated behind a policing or accountability term so the feed cannot fill with blotter.

Standing. Every wire is labelled by what kind of source it is, and every row carries the label. Oversight bodies & institutions is the treaty bodies, courts and complaints authorities — OHCHR, the European Court of Human Rights, the Inter-American Commission, the UN Working Group on Arbitrary Detention, national police complaints bodies. Research & forensic investigation is the institutes and open-source investigators: Citizen Lab, Forensic Architecture, Bellingcat, the Omega Research Foundation. Rights & civil liberties groups is the advocacy organisations — Amnesty, Human Rights Watch, EFF, Privacy International, Statewatch. Press is general news across 25 languages. Standing is provenance, not a claim that a story is important.

Weight. Separately, each story is scored on what it carries: a decision — a ruling, a law enacted, a conviction, a formal finding (2); institutional material — an oversight report, official statistics, a treaty body's guidance, a peer-reviewed study (2); a measured figure (1); a pending decision with a date attached (1); a named jurisdiction (1); and a primary source (1). At three or more the row is marked consequential, and the Weight filter narrows to those. A campaign statement and a court judgment both appear here; the pips are how you tell them apart at a glance, and the words beside them say what earned the score.

What is refused. Ordinary crime reporting with no policing-conduct or accountability angle, fiction and entertainment that uses the vocabulary — procedurals, film and television policing — and the usual commercial noise. The status line reports how many stories each harvest refused.

Translation. The Translate control at the top renders the headlines and snippets on screen into any of 26 languages, through a free public translation endpoint — no account, no key, nothing stored. A translated row is marked as machine translation and keeps the publisher's original wording on hover, because a machine rendering is not the publisher's words. If the endpoint is unavailable the row simply stays in its original language rather than emptying. Translation applies to what is on screen; scroll further or widen a filter and press the control again.

Region. Placement runs three levels deep. Pick a region and a second row opens with its subregions; pick one of those and a third row lists the places inside it — Latin America to the Amazon Basin to the Brazilian Amazon, Oceania to Australia to the Northern Territory. Each row counts what it holds, so an empty subregion is visible rather than hidden, and every story files under the subregion and region above whatever place it names.

Limits worth knowing. The gate is mechanical: it reads words, not meaning. Standing is assigned per wire rather than per article. Google News caps a query at roughly 100 results over about 30 days. Coverage is uneven by language and the counts show it rather than hiding it.

Every wire read on the last harvest, with its standing: what it returned, and how many of its stories were refused. A wire that could not be reached says so rather than disappearing.
